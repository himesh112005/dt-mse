1. Conceptual Overview

Ideation in Design Thinking relies on divergent exploration followed by convergent synthesis. Traditional brainstorming frequently suffers from cognitive biases, fear of criticism, and domination by vocal participants. These three methods counter these bottlenecks through unique creative pathways:

  • Silent Brainstorming (Brainwriting): Eliminates social anxiety and groupthink by separating idea generation from immediate verbal judgment through asynchronous, written inputs.
  • Metaphors (Analogical Thinking): Maps systemic structures from a well-understood domain (nature, daily habits) onto an unsolved engineering or design challenge.
  • What-If Scenarios (Provocative Inversion): Deliberately removes core constraints, technical bottlenecks, or standard economic rules to force radical thinking.

CO-03: Subjective 6-Mark Questions (Complete Reference)

Q1. Discuss Big Picture Thinking using System Operator and Function Modelling. How do these tools help in understanding complex design problems holistically?

System Operator (9-Windows): Maps any system across 3 hierarchical levels (Supersystem, System, Subsystem) and 3 temporal horizons (Past, Present, Future). It guarantees designers don't solve micro-problems while creating macro-failures.

Function Modelling: Visualizes technical interactions between components (useful, harmful, insufficient functions) to eliminate defects at the root level.

Q2. What is TRIZ (Theory of Inventive Problem Solving)? Describe core principles and how Inventive Principles are applied in engineering design innovation.

TRIZ Foundations: Formulated by Genrich Altshuller after studying 200,000+ patents. It proves that technical problems and contradictions repeat across industries and can be resolved using 40 standardized Inventive Principles.

Contradiction Matrix: Matches 39 engineering parameters (e.g., weight vs strength) without making compromises.

Q3. Explain the role of empathy in the Design Thinking process. How does empathy influence the definition of user problems and generation of innovative solutions?

Empathy as Foundation: Prevents designer bias. Through contextual observation and active listening, it uncovers latent and unstated user anxieties that cannot be captured by simple surveys.

Problem Reframing: Shifts problem statements from business-centric ("Increase subscription sales") to human-centric ("Provide effortless continuous skill tracking").

Q5. What are metaphors for ideation? Explain their importance with suitable examples.

Definition: Cognitive bridges that transfer structural and functional knowledge from a source domain to a target domain.

Examples: Velcro fastener (invented via burdock burrs clinging to dog fur); Shinkansen bullet train nose (redesigned using Kingfisher bird beak shape to prevent tunnel sonic booms).
